Never Dumb is the break feature built into Pomodorian: a set of short exercises that show up during your longer breaks so a few minutes belong to your own thinking instead of a feed. I built Never Dumb because I wanted my own breaks to be a moment of taking back control of my attention, not another five minutes of doomscrolling. If you want the reasoning and the research behind that choice, read [why active breaks beat doomscrolling](/blog/never-dumb-breaks) and [what cognitive debt is](/blog/cognitive-debt-ai-era). This guide is the practical one: what you'll actually see, screen by screen, and how to get the most out of it.

## When Never Dumb Appears

Never Dumb is tied to your long break, not every break. By default, Pomodorian gives you a long break after every 4th pomodoro (the interval is adjustable in Settings), and that's the only moment Never Dumb becomes eligible to open on its own.

Two things decide what happens next. If the "Auto-open Never Dumb on long breaks" toggle in Settings is on, which it is by default, the Never Dumb screen opens automatically the moment that long break starts running. If you switch modes by hand instead, say you jump straight to a long break from the mode selector without finishing your 4th pomodoro, the automatic pop-up won't fire. Manually changing modes resets the eligibility.

That second point doesn't lock you out, though. Any time the timer is in a running long break, whether you got there naturally or by switching modes yourself, a small "Run Never Dumb" button appears above the mode buttons. Click it and Never Dumb opens on demand. That includes right after you've skipped the automatic pop-up: the button comes back, and you can reopen it as many times as the break allows.

If you'd rather never see it pop up unprompted, or want to trigger it yourself every time, turn the Settings toggle off and rely on the button instead.

## Step by Step

### 1. The intro screen

![The Never Dumb intro screen, headline "Never dump your brain."](/screenshots/never-dumb.png)

The first thing you see is the intro: "Never dump your brain." with a short line about the philosophy underneath, and a link to the science article if you want it. From here, click "Run Never Dumb" to continue, or use the Skip button in the top right to bypass it entirely and take a normal break instead.

### 2. Choose a mode

![The Never Dumb mode screen, "What do you need?" with three cards for Serious, Fun, and Reset](/screenshots/never-dumb-modes.png)

Next comes "What do you need?", the three cards: Serious, Fun, and Reset. This is the real decision point.

- Pick **Serious** if you just came out of a focus block where an AI tool did a lot of the thinking (pairing on code, drafting a doc, debugging with a copilot) and you want to rebuild the reasoning yourself before it slips away.
- Pick **Fun** when you need to move and laugh more than you need to think. It trades depth for physical, slightly absurd movement.
- Pick **Reset** when you're saturated and a puzzle would just add more load. It's the mode that asks the least of your attention.

### 3. Do the exercise

![The Never Dumb serious menu, "Choose your resistance." with Rebuild, Break the Answer, and Compress](/screenshots/never-dumb-serious.png)

Say you pick Serious. You land on "Choose your resistance." with three cards: Rebuild, Break the Answer, Compress. Take Rebuild as an example: the instruction asks you to reconstruct, without reopening the AI chat, the three decisions you made in your last focus block, naming the decision, the reason behind it, and what would make you reverse it. You type your answer, set a rough confidence level, then hit Reveal to see the result screen. The whole point is doing the work before you see anything back.

Throughout every screen, a "Break remaining" countdown sits in the header. That's your actual break timer, still running underneath. Never Dumb doesn't pause it or add to it, it just gives the existing minutes something to do.

## The 13 Exercises at a Glance

| Mode | Exercise | Duration/Format | What it trains |
|---|---|---|---|
| Serious | Rebuild | Free text, no timer | Reconstructing your own reasoning after AI-assisted work |
| Serious | Break the Answer | Free text, no timer | Spotting a hidden logic error before reading the reveal |
| Serious | Compress | Free text, 25 words max | Keeping the constraint and the uncertainty, not just smoothing the prose |
| Fun | Human CAPTCHA | Physical mission, self-paced | Doing something a model can describe but not enjoy |
| Fun | Room Raid | Physical + recall, self-paced | Moving, noticing, and recalling without looking twice |
| Fun | Body Debugger | Physical, short timed sequence | Movement plus inhibition, ignoring the "AI SAYS" trap |
| Fun | Rubber Duck Boss | Spoken answers, 3 rounds | Explaining out loud before asking a machine to |
| Reset | Breathing 5-5 | 3 min | Downshifting pace with paced breathing |
| Reset | Open Meditation | 3 min | Noticing without fixing, no performance target |
| Reset | Body Scan | 3 min | Returning attention to the body |
| Reset | Five-minute Walk | 5 min | Changing the scene, screen-free |
| Reset | One-track Reset | 3 min | Listening to a local lofi track without a feed |

Fun mode draws from a shuffled queue of the four games rather than a fresh coin flip each time, so you'll see all four before any repeat, and never the same one twice back to back.

## Tips

- **Match the mode to your state, not your mood.** If you just closed a long AI pairing session, Serious does more for you than Fun, even if Fun sounds more appealing in the moment.
- **Save Reset for saturated days.** If the idea of one more decision, even a fake one, makes you tired, skip straight to Reset. It's the one mode designed to ask nothing back.
- **Give the toggle a week before judging it.** The auto-open can feel like an interruption the first few times. Since it only fires on long breaks, once every 4 pomodoros by default, a week of real use tells you more than the first pop-up does.
- **Use the manual button when you want it on your terms.** If you'd rather choose your moment than have it choose for you, turn the Settings toggle off and click "Run Never Dumb" only on the breaks where you actually want it.

## Key Takeaways

- Never Dumb only opens during long breaks, the ones that follow every 4th pomodoro by default, adjustable in Settings.
- Auto-open is on by default via the "Auto-open Never Dumb on long breaks" toggle; a manual "Run Never Dumb" button also appears during any running long break.
- The three modes serve different needs: Serious rebuilds reasoning, Fun trades depth for movement, Reset asks nothing of your attention.
- All 13 exercises are local only. Nothing you type is saved or sent anywhere.
- The break timer keeps running underneath the modal, and Skip or Escape gets you back to a normal break at any point.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I open Never Dumb manually?
Yes, within a long break. A "Run Never Dumb" button appears any time the timer is in a running long break, whether you got there naturally or by switching modes yourself, and it reappears even after you've skipped the automatic pop-up once. What you can't do yet is trigger it during a pomodoro or a short break, it's built around the long break specifically.

### Does Never Dumb save or send anything I type?
No. The footer on every screen says it directly: local only, nothing you type is saved or sent. Your answers exist for the length of the exercise and then they're gone.

### What happens to my break timer while I'm doing an exercise?
It keeps running. The "Break remaining" countdown in the header is the same clock as your actual break, so Never Dumb doesn't extend your break or eat into your next pomodoro. It just gives the existing minutes something to do.

### How do I turn off the automatic pop-up?
Open Settings and switch off "Auto-open Never Dumb on long breaks." It's on by default. Turning it off doesn't remove the feature: the "Run Never Dumb" button still shows up during long breaks whenever you want to open it yourself.

### Is Fun mode actually random?
Sort of. It cycles through the four games in a shuffled order rather than a fresh coin flip each time, so you'll never get the same one twice in a row, and you'll see all four before any of them repeats.
